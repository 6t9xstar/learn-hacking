# Books: Free, Open, and Worth Paying For

Books still carry the deepest explanations in this field. This page lists 30 titles: 14 that are free and legal to read online, and 16 paid books worth their price for a specific purpose. There are no pirated copies here, and there will not be.

> Ethics reminder: reading about exploitation does not authorise it. Apply what you learn only in labs you own or systems you are contracted to test. See [../../ETHICS.md](../../ETHICS.md).

## How this list is organised

- **Free and open first.** These are legally free from the author or publisher, not "free" via a file-sharing site.
- **Paid books are marked paid** and are listed without purchase links to avoid stale prices. Buy from the publisher or an official ebook store.
- **Every entry says why to read it and at what level.** A book that is wrong for your stage wastes weeks.

## How to read a technical book

1. **Read the first two chapters properly.** They set vocabulary and assumptions.
2. **Skim the middle.** Look for the chapter that solves your current problem.
3. **Do the exercises, or build a small equivalent.** Reading about memory corruption without debugging a crash does not transfer.
4. **Write a one-page summary.** If you cannot summarise it, you did not read it, you scanned it.
5. **Revisit after six months.** Sections you skipped become relevant once you have context.

For a beginner, two free books (The Linux Command Line plus one field-specific title) are worth more than ten paid books read passively.

## Where to buy paid books legitimately

- The publisher's own site, which often has DRM-free ebooks and bundles.
- An official ebook store or your local bookshop.
- Your library, including interlibrary loan and institutional digital access.
- Publisher subscription services, if your employer or school provides them.

If a copy is free on a file-sharing site, it is piracy. Do not link it, do not use it, and do not accept it as a contribution to this repository. See [../../CONTRIBUTING.md](../../CONTRIBUTING.md).

## Building a reading habit

- **One technical book at a time.** Parallel reading produces parallel abandonment.
- **Twenty pages a day** beats a weekend binge for retention.
- **Keep a quote file.** One sentence per chapter, in your own words.
- **Pair each book with a lab.** The book explains; the lab verifies.
- **Abandon bad books early.** A dated or badly edited book costs more than it returns.
