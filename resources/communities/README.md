# Communities, Conferences, and Newsletters

Security is a field where the community is part of the curriculum. Good communities answer specific questions, correct bad habits early, and connect you to people who have solved your problem before. Bad ones normalise recklessness. This page lists 18 places worth your attention and explains how to participate without damaging your reputation or someone else's systems.

> Ethics reminder: a community is not an authorisation. Asking "how do I hack this site" about a system you do not own will get you banned at best. See [../../ETHICS.md](../../ETHICS.md).

## At a glance

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Cost | Free, except conference tickets |
| Time commitment | 30 minutes a week for newsletters; a few hours a month for communities |
| Best entry point | One forum, one newsletter, one local meetup |
| Value | Corrections, context, job leads, and mentorship |
| Risk | Misinformation, scope creep, and burnout |

| Community | Type | Where | Best for | Signal-to-noise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| [r/netsec](https://www.reddit.com/r/netsec/) | Moderated forum | Reddit | Technical research, tool releases, and disclosure discussion | High, heavily moderated |

| [r/blueteamsec](https://www.reddit.com/r/blueteamsec/) | Discussion forum | Reddit | Detection engineering, DFIR, and defensive tooling | High |

| [SANS Internet Storm Center](https://isc.sans.edu/) | Community diaries and forums | Web | Daily threat observations and practitioner Q&A | High |

Reddit is useful if you treat it as a searchable archive. Search before posting; most beginner questions have been answered well several times.

## Conferences and villages

| Event or community | Type | Typical cadence | Best for | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [DEF CON](https://defcon.org/) | Conference with villages and contests | Annual, late summer | Immersion: villages, hardware, social engineering, CTFs | Paid ticket |
| [Black Hat](https://www.blackhat.com/) | Professional conference | Annual, multiple regions | Deep technical briefings and training | Paid |
| [Security BSides](https://bsides.org/) | Community conferences | Many local events year-round | A first conference, local hiring, approachable talks | Free or low cost |
| [OWASP Global AppSec](https://owasp.org/events/) | Application security conference | Annual, rotating regions | Web and application security depth | Paid, with some free content |
| [Blue Team Village](https://blueteamvillage.org/) | Conference village and community | Year-round online plus DEF CON | Defensive practitioners, detection, DFIR | Mostly free |
| [Red Team Village](https://redteamvillage.io/) | Conference village and community | Year-round online plus DEF CON | Offensive tradecraft and career guidance | Mostly free |
| [Women in CyberSecurity (WiCyS)](https://www.wicys.org/) | Professional association | Annual conference plus local groups | Mentorship, recruiting, and community for women and allies | Membership and event fees |

BSides is the highest-value first conference: inexpensive, local, and full of people who remember being new. Villages are the cheapest way to try hardware, lockpicking, or social engineering in a controlled setting.

## Newsletters, podcasts, and reporting

| Source | Format | Cadence | Best for | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [tl;dr sec](https://tldrsec.com/) | Newsletter | Weekly | A curated summary of security research and tooling | Free, with a paid tier |
| [Risky Business](https://risky.biz/) | Podcast and newsletter | Multiple per week | Industry news with sceptical, well-informed commentary | Free, with paid extras |
| [Darknet Diaries](https://darknetdiaries.com/) | Podcast | Monthly-ish | Narrative stories that explain why security matters | Free |
| [The CyberWire](https://thecyberwire.com/) | Newsletter and podcast | Daily | Concise news briefings for situational awareness | Free, with paid tiers |
| [Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/) | Investigative blog | Irregular | Deep reporting on cybercrime and fraud ecosystems | Free |

Pick at most two. A daily news habit can feel productive while teaching you very little; a weekly curated newsletter plus one narrative source is usually enough.

## Safety and professionalism

Community participation has consequences that outlast the thread.

- **Never ask for help attacking a system you do not own.** "How do I get into this site" is a request for unauthorised access. Ask about the technique in the abstract, in a lab context.
- **Do not post stolen data, credentials, or personal information**, even as "evidence". You may be committing a further offence and you expose innocent people.
- **Do not name and shame.** If you believe a company is vulnerable, follow coordinated disclosure: contact them privately, give a reasonable deadline, and escalate to a CERT or the platform's security contact if they do not respond.
- **Respect codes of conduct.** Conferences publish them for a reason; harassment and boundary-pushing are not "just the culture".
- **Assume screenshots are permanent.** Blur tokens, hostnames, and personal data before posting.
- **Do not share other people's paid material.** Course dumps and exam questions are prohibited here and are a fast route to a ban.
- **Check local law before you publish.** Some jurisdictions treat proof-of-concept details differently; a lawyer's advice beats a forum consensus.
- **Protect your identity where it matters.** Use a separate handle for research if your employer or clients could be affected by association.
- **Do not dox yourself by accident.** Metadata in screenshots, hostnames, and timestamps reveals more than people expect.

## How to ask a question that gets answered

1. State what you are trying to do, in one sentence.
2. State what you expected and what happened, with the exact error text.
3. Show what you already tried, including the commands.
4. Say what you have read, so people do not repeat it.
5. Keep it short; a wall of text gets skimmed.

A good question is a small piece of documentation. The people most likely to help you are the ones who can answer in one message.

## How to contribute back

- **Answer one question a week** once you are a few months in. Explaining something is the fastest way to find gaps in your own understanding.
- **Write up your dead ends.** "This did not work and here is why" is more useful than another success story.
- **Review other people's write-ups.** Careful, kind correction raises the quality of the whole archive.
- **Speak at a local meetup.** A 15-minute talk on a lab you completed is enough for a BSides-style event.

## Avoiding burnout

- **Mute the firehose.** Turn off notifications for anything that is not a direct message.
- **Follow people, not platforms.** A handful of practitioners who explain their reasoning beats a thousand headlines.
- **Accept that you cannot read everything.** Nobody does; the ones who claim to are skimming.
- **Take breaks without guilt.** The field will still be there, and it will still be behind.

## Reporting and disclosure etiquette

- **Give a private channel a chance first.** Most organisations have a security contact or a disclosure policy.
- **Be precise and reproducible.** A report with steps and impact gets fixed; a vague warning gets ignored.
- **Set a reasonable timeline.** Ninety days is a common norm; shorter for actively exploited issues.
- **Do not escalate by public shaming.** Coordinated disclosure exists because public pressure can harm users who have no patch yet.
- **Never demand payment.** Bug bounty terms are set by the programme, not by the researcher.

## Next steps

1. Join one community and one newsletter, not five.
2. Find your nearest OWASP chapter or BSides event and attend one meeting.
3. Ask one well-formed question this month, and answer one next month.
4. Read the [certifications guide](../certifications/README.md) for how community reputation interacts with credentials.
5. Check [../../CONTRIBUTING.md](../../CONTRIBUTING.md) before contributing material to this repository.
